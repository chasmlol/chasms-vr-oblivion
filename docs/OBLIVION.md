# Oblivion in the Chasms VR Framework

The adapter (`crates/oblivion`, built as a 32-bit `dinput8.dll` beside `Oblivion.exe`) puts
Oblivion's world in the headset and the framework's player in it. Oblivion runs its world, and the
runtime runs the player: the head, the hands, the body and what they hold. The adapter only
translates. Where the adapter hooks the game is at the end, under "Hooks".

## How it works

| Part | What it does | Where |
|---|---|---|
| The picture | Oblivion's Direct3D 9 device is made on D3D9On12. After each eye's render, the back buffer goes through a shared Direct3D 12 texture and fence into the runtime's eye texture. The game's textures are filtered anisotropically (`anisotropy`: Oblivion has no filtering of its own, and a driver's override doesn't reach D3D9On12), and an indexed draw from a vertex buffer in system memory has its vertex range widened to the whole buffer, as Direct3D 9's drivers read it (D3D9On12 copies only the range the draw names, and the rest was stale: streaks across the trees). | `d3d9.rs`, `sysmem.rs` |
| The eyes | The world render runs once per eye each frame, with the camera at that eye's pose and the eye's own asymmetric frustum. The game's first-person arms aren't drawn. The sky is centred on each eye (at infinity for both) and the weather on the head; each eye is a frame of the renderer's own, so the actors' skinned bodies are made for its camera; the actors' shadow maps are made once for both eyes, their clip planes again for each. What else the game makes once a frame: `docs/design/visuals.md`. | `render.rs`, `camera.rs` |
| Resolution | The game renders each eye at the size the runtime asks for: the headset's recommended size times the Display tab's Supersampling, bigger again with its Overscan (drawn past the headset's view). The window, back buffer and every render target are made at that size, put in the game's screen size and `iSize W`/`iSize H` as it makes its window. The game can't change its size while it runs, so the size asked for is kept beside the game (`rustvr-eye-size.txt`): a game started before the headset is on starts at the last one, and a change in the menu takes at the next start (until then the runtime shows only as much overscan as the size keeps sharp). `eye_width` and `eye_height` fix the size. | `resolution.rs` |
| Depth | Each eye's depth buffer (resolved from its samples under the game's antialiasing) goes to the runtime as linear depth, so the hands, the body and what they hold are hidden behind the world. | `d3d9.rs`, `depth.rs` |
| Collision | The hands, the arms and what they hold meet what's drawn: each object near the player whose model has collision of its own (buildings, rocks, furniture, shelves, statues) is given the runtime as its model's drawn shape, less what isn't solid (glows, water, cloth, leaves, cobwebs, what its animations move), worked out from its model in the game's archives as it comes near; the land, trees and what draws nothing keep Oblivion's own Havok shapes. The game's arrows in flight meet the drawn shapes too: through the gaps the game's collision fills, into the drawn surface with the game's own impact (`arrows.rs`). The game's own walking and physics keep the game's collision. `oblivion-extract hitboxes` checks every model's hitbox against the game's collision. Research and design: [design/hitboxes.md](design/hitboxes.md). | `collision.rs`, `arrows.rs`, `oblivion_assets::hitbox` |
| The HUD | After the eyes, the game draws its HUD and menus into an alpha render target of ours (not its back buffer), alpha blended as coverage: the HUD texture is the UI premultiplied, over nothing. The HUD is cut from its own menus' tiles: the bars, the weapon and the spell, the compass and the active effects over the off-hand wrist while its palm is turned up; the reticle's activate icon and the sneak eye, and the card of what it's on, where the head looks (the crosshair itself is hidden: the hands aim, and the icon is the game's only sign of a theft before it's done); a foe's health over the foe; subtitles and notices on the caption strip. Outside the world, the whole picture is a menu panel. | `render.rs`, `d3d9.rs`, `hud.rs`, `tiles.rs`, `reticle.rs`, `hud_info.rs` |
| The player | Before each game frame, the player faces where the head looks (rotation and pitch). A third-person view goes back to first person. | `player.rs` |
| Walking | On the ground the player's step is the stick's: whichever way it points, at the game's own pace for how the player moves now (walking, running, sneaking, laden) times how far it's pushed, and no step once it's let go (the game's own move given the step; its own walking goes eight ways and strides on 10 to 20 cm after the keys are let go). Jumping, falling and swimming stay the game's. In VR the player's character controller, and those of the people near them, are a body's round (the game's own `SetSize`), and a step stops where it would touch another's: up to people, not shoving them. Where the game's wider body is held up (at a table, its edge 30 cm from the eyes), the view goes on as far as the player's own body has room: up to a table until their front is at it, a head's breadth from a wall. | `movement.rs`, `input.rs` |
| Frame time | Oblivion times each frame by `GetTickCount`, a clock that moves every 15.6 ms: at a headset's 90 to 120 frames a second most frames were timed at nothing and every second or third at 15 ms, and the world lurched. The frame's time is the real time since the last frame, from the performance counter (in the game's clock update); the game's pause, fixed step, 166 ms cap and time scale stay its own. Its Havok steps once every frame by that frame's time (update type 10, unsmoothed), not in fixed steps of 1/60 s. | `clock.rs`, `player.rs` |
| Sneaking | Crouching a little for real is Oblivion's own Sneak, with everything the game does with it: the runtime presses the game's Crouch/Sneak toggle as the head goes under its standing height by the Comfort tab's "Stealth crouch amount" (8 cm by default) and again as it comes back up (never on a horse or in the water), and keeps its crouch in step with the game's sneaking. The sneak's speed is the game's. Oblivion lowers its first-person camera 35 cm in a sneak: in one a real crouch started, that drop is left out of the headset's view, which stays where the head is; a sneak from the button is the flat game's, the view going down with it. See [design/stealth.md](design/stealth.md). | `camera.rs`, `link.rs`, `render.rs`; the runtime's `mechanics.rs` |
| Input | The game's DirectInput keyboard and mouse read the runtime's actions, through the player's own `Oblivion.ini` bindings. Walking is the game's own analog joystick walking: the stick is written into its first joystick's axes after it polls its devices. While in VR the real keyboard and mouse don't reach the game. | `input.rs` |
| The window | It's always "active", so the game never pauses for focus: with another window in front (the desktop view, OBS) the game plays on, its clock never paused for it, its controls the headset's, its sounds still heard (every sound buffer made with global focus). In the test mode it's off-screen and never activated. | `window.rs` |
| The desktop view | The runtime's window on the monitor, for recording and streaming (on by default, fullscreen; F11 switches): a steadied camera between the eyes on one eye's picture (the right's by default, the other filling in), a flat game's field of view (105 degrees; 65 while a bow is drawn to the cheek), the horizon level. It bobs with Oblivion's own first-person bob (how the game's first-person animations move its camera, without the sneak's posture; on a horse, the seat's), the runtime's own, or none (the Studio tab). The Studio tab's looks and effects (motion blur, depth of field, shake, the view widening with speed, the lens, grain, colour grades, a chest mount, smoothed snap turns, the game's HUD where a flat player has it) are the desktop view's alone: the headset's picture never changes. See [design/view-bob.md](design/view-bob.md). | `camera.rs`, `link.rs`; the runtime's `spectator.rs`, `spectator_fx.rs`, `view_bob.rs` |
| Starting | Starting Oblivion starts the runtime: `rustvr-runtime.txt` beside the DLL (the installer writes it) names `rustvr-play.exe`, which is started in the background with no test range (`--no-range`) and quits a few seconds after the game does. Nothing is started when a runtime is already running or the file isn't there. | `launcher.rs` |
| Settings | `Oblivion.ini` is windowed while in VR and skips the intro films (a test run, the logo before the main menu and a new game's film too), and a few of the game's own settings are raised to what a headset needs, a player's higher values kept: the actors' shadow maps at 1024, no negative mip bias on the trees and their full detail, the water's waves at 36 frames a second, a 250 MB preload, things, items and actors fading out at their furthest, the trees, the grass (to 57 m) and the distant land further out, and faces, lip files, the morpher, blood and path finding on the game's background threads ([design/oblivion-image-quality.md](design/oblivion-image-quality.md)). The player's values are kept (`rustvr-oblivion-ini.txt`) and put back when the game closes, or at the next start after a crash. | `game_settings.rs` |
| xOBSE | When xOBSE is installed beside the game, its Steam loader (`obse_steam_loader.dll`) is loaded where xOBSE's own launcher would load it: on the game's main thread before `WinMain`, at the C runtime's call of `GetCommandLineA` (its import ours until then). Started from its exe rather than through Steam, the game would otherwise load neither xOBSE nor its plugins (Oblivion Stutter Remover). | `xobse.rs` |
| The body | The runtime draws the player's body from the game's own models: the race's skin where nothing is worn, the armour and clothes worn over it, on the game's skeleton. The head: the race's head and the parts made to it (ears, mouth, teeth, tongue, eyes), the hair (unless a helmet or hood covers its slot) and what's worn on the head, each with the race's, the eyes' or the hair's own texture. The face: FaceGen's coordinates (the race's plus the NPC's), the age map FaceGen's age picks, the hair's colour; the runtime morphs the head's parts and makes its texture as the game does. In first person the head stays out of the player's eyes. Its joints keep their volume, and the armour's plates move as the hard things they are, as `content/armour/oblivion.toml` marks them (every vanilla set; the framework's `docs/ARMOUR.md`, `docs/design/armour.md`). | `body.rs`, `docs/design/mirror.md`, `content/armour/oblivion.toml` |
| The mirror | A standing mirror the player calls up: with the Mirror, a lesser power every player has (a created spell the saves keep, cast as any power; the mirror arrives as a summoning does, Summon Dremora's effect at its foot; cast looking at a mirror, it sends that one away), the runtime's Body tab, or pulling the gripped hands apart in front of the chest. The game's world in it is one more render a frame from the head reflected in the glass, its near plane on the glass, copied with its depth; the runtime draws each eye's reflection with the player in it. | `render.rs`, `d3d9.rs`, `mirror_spell.rs` |
| The race menu | Opened by a new game or `showracemenu`: the world stays round the player, a mirror stands in front of them and the menu (the part of the picture its tiles take) beside it, worked by the laser. The game's own third-person player is hidden; every change in the menu (race, sex, face, hair, eyes) shows on the mirrored body as it's made. | `menus.rs`, `hud_info.rs`, `link.rs` |
| The face | In the race menu the face is shaped by hand: a palm near it (on a bust of the player's own head, 1.7 times life size, in front of them to the right, or on their own head) shows in gold what its grip would take, and the grip pulls it through the face's own FaceGen coordinates, within the race's sliders' ranges; the trigger squeezed while pulling makes it one-sided. Each face goes into the character as the menu's own sliders put it (the character's coordinates, the game's own heads morphed), the sliders follow it, and Undo and Reset buttons made from the menu's own are on its pages. See [design/facesculpt.md](design/facesculpt.md). | `facesculpt.rs`; the runtime's `sculpt.rs` |
| Lighting | The body and what the hands hold are lit as Oblivion's own shaders light them: the scene's sun or moon, the point lights near the player (torches, candles, spells), the ambient (Night-Eye's too), normal maps, specular, the skin's rim, glow maps and the fog. | `lighting.rs` |
| Fog | The game's vertex shaders fog by how far away a thing is, not by the length of its clip-space position, which made the headset's wide, off-centre eyes each fog a far hill differently. Each shader that fogs is rewritten as the game makes it, and each camera rendered with gives it its frustum. `RUSTVR_OBLIVION_GAME_FOG=1` keeps the game's own, to compare. | `fog.rs`, `d3d9.rs`, `render.rs` |
| Trees and grass | Grass grows all round the player, not only in the cone ahead of a flat screen's camera, out to the game's grass distance, built again when the player has moved and not when the head turns. The distant land's tree cards are turned to the line from the eye to each tree, not to where the camera looks, so the far forest doesn't shimmer as the head turns (a shader of ours given in place of `DISTLOD2003.vso` as the game makes it). The trees' leaf cards turn as the player walks round a tree, not as the head turns or leans, and a leaf shader's constants that aren't finite are made 0 (Direct3D 9 multiplied them away; D3D9On12 makes NaN of them). `RUSTVR_OBLIVION_GAME_GRASS`, `RUSTVR_OBLIVION_GAME_DISTANT_TREES` and `RUSTVR_OBLIVION_GAME_LEAF_FACING` (`=1`) keep the game's own. See `docs/design/visuals.md`. | `grass.rs`, `distant_trees.rs`, `leaves.rs`, `d3d9.rs` |
| Weapons | The equipped weapon (the game's model) hangs at the left hip, or over the right shoulder if two-handed (a staff too), and the hand draws it. The equipped shield is taken from over the left shoulder. Edges, points, heads and guards are measured on each model. | `weapons.rs` |
| Archery | The equipped bow is the runtime's bow (the game's model, measured on it, bending as the game's own morph bends it, its limbs springing back after a shot as the player's bow animation has them), on the back over the left shoulder; the arrows come from whichever of the body's slots they're kept in (the holsters' Arrows slot over the right shoulder, where Oblivion wears its quiver, to begin with): the kind on the bow as the bow's own arrows (their model, the equipped stack's count), another kind equipped by the game as it comes out and then the bow's own in the hand. The body wears the quiver with as many of its five arrows showing as are left. An arrow loosed is the game's own `ArrowProjectile` from the VR arrow's tip along it, with the power the game gives its bow held drawn that long (the draw stands in for the hold timer), then the game's `UseAmmo`, fatigue, invisibility and wear; `WPNBowDraw` and `bowShoot` play at the bow, and held drawn the fatigue burns as the game's hold burns it. The arrow flies, hits, sticks and is picked up as the game's own. See [design/archery.md](design/archery.md) | `archery.rs`, `body.rs` |
| Combat | Hits are the game's own hit with the equipped weapon (damage, skill, enchantments). A blow met by the weapon or shield is the game's block. Spells charge in the palm and go off as the game's own cast: target spells from the palm along the aim, touch spells into what the hand touches, self spells on the player. An enchanted staff casts from its tip along its length as the game's own staff cast (its charge used up, or the game's "not enough charge"). | `combat.rs` |
| Magic | The game's own casting art where the hand casts: the spell's main effect's model playing its `SpecialIdle_Cast` art (fire's flames, frost's mist, healing's sparkles), its first part held burning in the palm while the spell is readied (small) and charging (growing), with the effect's casting light fading in and its casting sound as the charge starts; a staff's enchantment the same at its tip. An enchanted weapon or staff shimmers with its enchantment's own effect shader (its membrane, drawn over the held model), and a spell's shader on the player (healing's glow, a shield's flames, being burned) is drawn over the body and hands, through the game's own fade. | `magic.rs`, `scene.rs`, `docs/design/torches.md` |
| Torch | The equipped torch is on the body as the equipped weapon is, flame up and burning: drawn from a holsters slot (the Torches category), the game equips it and it takes the copy's place in the hand; equipped from the game's menu, it starts at the right hip. Either hand draws it, waves it, holsters it in any holster and drops it (it lies burning where it lands, as a sword let go lies). The game draws it: a copy of its model with the game's flame, its light made from the torch's form (radius, colour, attenuation) and flickered as the game flickers a torch, lighting the world, the body and the hands; it crackles with the game's held-torch loop. | `torch.rs`, `scene.rs` |
| Grabbing people | A hand on any part of an actor is the game's own grab: a `bhkMouseSpringAction` on the Havok body of the bone held, at the point held, pulled where the hand would have it with the hand's pull (in the game's masses: its ragdolls weigh about eight times a person, measured on the player's own). A dead or knocked-down body drags by any part. A living one keeps animating: the held limb's bones go physical (their blend objects' gains), the spine partly, and blend back when let go; pulled along the ground it's pushed after the pull (its character controller's push). Lifted by the head, neck or torso, or yanked (a leg trips sooner), it's knocked down (the game's `KnockbackActor`) and hangs from the hand, kept from getting up while held; let go, it gets up the game's way. The throat squeezed drains fatigue until the game knocks it out; held up by the throat it claws at the hand and kicks. Choking, knocking down, throwing or holding a living actor long is the game's `OnAttacked` from the player (its assault crime and bounty, combat). An open hand pushes a body that's all physics, and a hand coming in fast shoves a standing one. | `grabs.rs`, `docs/design/npcs.md` |
| Creatures and gore | Every actor the game fully processes near the player is capsules on its own bones for the hands and blades, each as thick as what it wears there (measured on the models drawn on it: a helmet's crown, a gauntlet's fingers, a cuirass over the hips), so a hand meets the armour, not the body inside it. Each bleeds the game's own blood for it (its decals and spray: people's red, a lich's black, a will-o'-the-wisp's glow, a skeleton's bone dust). An edge's killing blow, or a cut into a corpse, strong enough for the limb (the framework's rule, `rustvr_protocol::severs`, with the game's damage for the weapon) takes it off at the joint the blade crossed (the neck, a shoulder, elbow, wrist, hip, knee or ankle): the body's bones past it shrunk to nothing there, the limb a clone of the creature's 3D that falls, rolls and can be taken, the stump spraying in pulses. See [design/gore.md](design/gore.md). | `shapes.rs`, `combat.rs`, `gore.rs` |
| Looking at the player | People look at the player's real head, the headset, not the game's own eye: every look at the player (idle head tracking, being spoken to, the dialogue menu, greetings, combat, the voice's listeners) is aimed at it (the player's look-at point), and in the dialogue menu, where the game may not run its head tracking, whoever looks at the player is aimed at it each frame. | `look.rs`, `docs/design/npcs.md` |
| Things in the world | Everything the player can take near them (weapons, armour, clothes, books, potions, ingredients, keys, soul gems, sigil stones, apparatus, carriable lights, clutter) is the hands' to take or pull with the gloves: its collision its drawn shape's convex parts (its Havok shape until they're worked out), knocked about by a hand or what it holds (the game's Havok given the push), its weight its Havok mass, drawn by the game where the hand has it (a candle still burns), and let go, the game's again, flying on with the throw. A card beside it says what it is: its name, damage or armour, weight, value, condition, and whether it's stolen. Let go in any body slot (a hip, a shoulder, the chest, the belt), the player takes it: the game's own pick-up, with its stealing and its message. | `items.rs`, `objects.rs` |
| Chains and ropes | The world's loose Havok bodies the player can't take (hanging chains and the chain dolls on them, roots, ropes, a bucket or skulls on a rope, a trap's chain once it's sprung) are solid to the hands, which rest on them and push them, and the game's Havok swings them on their own constraints. Taken in a hand, one is held with the game's own grab (a `bhkMouseSpringAction`, as holding Activate on a thing does): a chain taut on its anchor holds the hand back, and the hand comes off it pulled 30 cm further. | `props.rs` |
| Doors and levers | Every door near the player that swings or slides in place (not a load door) and every scripted activator with a moving part (levers, switches, buttons, wheels) is a hinge or a slide in the runtime's physics (`OBJ_VALVE`, `JOINT_PANEL`): the joint from the model's own "Open" or "Forward" animation (read from the game's archives), its shape its drawn shape for the hands and the game's arrows (a gate's bars, not the slab of its Havok shape; the Havok shape until the drawn one is worked out), its mass the model's or its drawn volume of its Havok material (a lid no heavier than a hand lifts by its edge; a button against a firm spring). Taken anywhere and swung, pushed with a palm, stopped by the player's body. A shut door is latched: a tug to the latch's end is the game's own Activate (its script, lock and key, open state); opened, the game's animation is stopped and the door's nodes and keyframed body follow the hand; swung shut, the game's Activate again. A lever's end of travel is the game's Activate (an animation its script plays in answer is stopped: the hand moved it). A sliding door (a portcullis, a ruin's stone) taken in a hand is tugged: the game's animation moves it. Traps and their triggers stay the game's. The game moving one itself (an NPC, the trigger at gaze) plays its animation and the runtime follows. | `world.rs`, `docs/design/world.md` |
| Talking | Optional, for power users: the framework's voice plugin (`rustvr-voice`, built on m8; not in the default build, built with `-p rustvr-voice`; the runtime starts `rustvr-voice.exe` from beside itself while the Voice tab's "Talk to the game's characters out loud" is on). It needs its servers (m8's speech to text and Breeze's voices, started by `clips/voice-servers.ps1`) and an LLM key of the player's own (`%LOCALAPPDATA%\RustVR\voice.toml`'s `[llm]`: `api_key`, or `api_key_env` naming a variable). With it, the player talks out loud to whoever they look at, within 5 m: they turn to listen as the player starts, and answer in a voice cloned from the game's own lines for them (their own actor's, else their race and sex's), saying it as the game's speakers do (the game's own speech: the plugin's mouth shapes on their face, their expression, the subtitle, the 3D voice from them). Who they are is the game's: name, race, class, factions, disposition, where they are, what they're doing, and what its dialogue has them say. Each one's memory goes with the saves. See [design/voice.md](design/voice.md). Simple voice commands need no plugin and are on by default (the Voice tab): Windows' own speech recognition hears the words of the menu on top (a dialogue's topics, "Goodbye") and clicks them, and to someone looked at, "what have you got for sale" opens their barter and "can we talk" their dialogue. | `voice.rs`, `commands.rs` |
| The lock | While the game's lock is open, the right hand is its pick: sideways under a tumbler, up to push it, the trigger to set it (the game's cursor and mouse, so every rule is the game's); A is Auto Attempt, B closes it; the tumbler, a set and a broken pick are felt. | `lock.rs` |
| Menus | The world goes on being rendered under menus (the game's frozen picture of it is skipped), and every menu floats in it in pieces of its own UI, read from its tiles: a container's list, tabs and buttons beside the container; barter beside the merchant; dialogue at the speaker (the name over their head, the line under their face, the topics beside them); the big four ahead of the player with the HUD's switcher; questions and counts where the eyes look; services from dialogue beside the speaker; the race menu beside the mirror; the pause menu ahead, with RustVR's own settings beside it; anything else ahead, cut out of the screen. The parchment is solid as the game draws it (`menu_background` makes it see-through). The game's cursor follows the laser (with a tick in the hand as the game's hover moves onto another thing), the trigger clicks, the right stick turns its mouse wheel. See `docs/design/menus.md`. | `hud.rs`, `tiles.rs`, `menus.rs`, `render.rs` |
| The main menu | The main menu is in a real place in Cyrodiil: at the main menu with no game, the game's own `coc` (or `cow`), `setpos`, `setangle`, `set gamehour` and `fw` take the boot's default player to a spot players call one of Oblivion's most beautiful views (the Imperial City across Lake Rumare at sunset, by default), and the main menu stays open over it, the world drawn under it in stereo. The place lives (the sky, water and wind with the time passing) while the hour and everyone in it stay still. The menu's logo and words float out over the view beside its subject, in the HUD's cream and shadowed Kingthings, worked by the laser through the game's own cursor, hover (its brush-stroke and sound) and click; New goes through the game's own exit to the main menu first, so it's a clean game. The hands alone are drawn. See `docs/design/worldmenu.md`. | `worldmenu.rs`, `hud.rs` |
| Loading | Loading never leaves the world: while the game loads another place, the last picture of the world is held round the player by the runtime, reprojected with the head by its depth (`SLOT_HELD`), and only the loading screen's bar floats ahead (its black, picture and title page cleared). The game's own fade after a load is the handover: the held place fades out as the new one fades in, as much as the game's fader says. | `render.rs`, `hud.rs` |
| Riding | The game's horses near the player offer their saddles (measured on the game's `saddle.nif`, on each horse's live spine): a hand closing on one is the player's own activation of the horse, so the game's mount happens (whose horse, the crime of stealing it, its animation and sounds). Riding, the runtime's reins, stick and spurs give the game its own controls: the pace is the joystick's Y and Run (a share of a gallop made from the game's own walk and run speeds for that horse), the turn the game's own turning of the horse's heading at the reins' share of its turn rate, and Jump the jump; the horse's turn carries the view round, the view's anchor is the rider's eye held steady on it, and the body sits in the saddle with its legs round the horse's own body. Getting off is the same activation. No fighting from the saddle (the game's rule). | `riding.rs`, `docs/design/riding.md` |
| Books and notes | A book or note lying about, taken in a hand, is read at once: the game reads it as activating it does (its book menu, a skill book's skill rise, a note's script, its Take with the stealing), once while it's held (a magic scroll isn't: the game would take it). The book menu floats over the book in the hand, turned to the eyes however the hand turns it, steadied (no shake with the hand), the game's two pages tipped a little about the spine (a note's scroll in one piece), drawn with the hands in their depth; the free hand's grip near the pages, dragged across them, turns them (a page every 5 cm; the game's own page step and sound), and its fingertip presses the buttons (Take, Exit, the page arrows), no laser. Let go of, or Exit: shut (it opens again once it's let go of and taken again). It isn't a menu: opened with no menu up it's kept off the interface's menu stack, so the game plays on (walking, fighting, the HUD but what the book covers), and its pages and buttons are worked on the menu itself (its own page step and click), the game's mouse being its play. A book activated on a shelf by looking at it comes to the off hand and opens there; one read from the inventory is held in the off hand like a sheet. See `docs/design/books.md`. | `books.rs`, `items.rs`, `hud.rs` |
| The map | Off for now: the folded map is on the body only with `map_slot` set (the body's slots are the holsters'). Kept there and drawn from it, the game's map opens (the local map inside, the world map outside) and its page and tabs are held in the hand like paper, turned to the eyes; the other hand's grip on its far side spreads it between the hands (bigger as they part), its grip on the face drags the map (the game's own pan), and its fingertip presses the tabs and places, no laser (fast travel, with the game's own question and rules). Put back or let go, it goes back to the hip and the map shuts. | `map.rs`, `hud.rs` |
| Holsters | The player's things in slots on the body: the inventory read with the game's own rows, in categories (food, potions, poisons, ingredients, scrolls, arrows, weapons, soul gems, keys, books, sigil stones, apparatus, misc, torches), each with what its effects restore, its value, weight and damage as the inventory shows them, and its model (`meshes\clutter` exported). A hand squeezing in a slot takes its first thing (by the slot's categories and order: most healing first, most valuable...) as the game's model in the fist, with the inventory's own sound. | Pockets | While the player sneaks, the NPCs within 3 m (alive, not fighting) wear pockets on their own bones: the trouser pockets at the front and the back of each hip, reached from in front, from behind or from the side. A thing pulled out is the game's own attempt: its chance (both Sneaks with Luck, the value taken) against its roll, then its own transfer (stolen, its message) or its own crime and reaction (bounty, disposition, the victim's alarm, never robbable again). | Alchemy | Every mortar and pestle near the player is a bench: the runtime draws it as two pieces from the game's model (the mortar, a bowl the hands use; the pestle, the hands' own), the game's own model hidden. | Swimming | The game's water goes to the runtime each frame: the cell's water height, whether the game has the player swimming and their head under, breath against its maximum, Water Walking, and the game's own swim speed (Speed, Athletics, Swim Speed effects, load), with the water's colour and fog. ## Setting up

The game's models are converted once into `local_assets/oblivion` (never committed; the clutter is
what's in people's pockets):

```
cargo run --release -p oblivion-assets --bin oblivion-extract -- export meshes/weapons meshes/staffs meshes/lights meshes/characters meshes/armor meshes/clothes meshes/creatures/horse textures/characters textures/menus/misc/hud_reticle.dds meshes/clutter meshes/oblivion/sigil meshes/dungeons/misc
```

and its sounds, as the runtime's sound pack (`local_assets/oblivion/sounds`):

```
cargo run --release -p oblivion-assets --bin oblivion-extract -- sounds
```

(`meshes/characters` brings the races' heads and the parts made to them, the hair and their FaceGen
morphs, `morphs/*.rvf`, and FaceGen's age control, `facegen.toml`.)

Bows are written bent too (`#bent`, from their morph), arrows alone (`#arrow`: the plain key is
the whole model, quiver and all) and their quivers worn on the skeleton (`#quiver0` ..
`#quiver5`), with `bows.toml` (the limbs' spring, from the player's bow animation); the reticle is
the bow's optional crosshair. `meshes/creatures/horse` measures the saddle.

For alchemy, the mortars and pestles split and measured, and the game's fonts and menu art for its
cards and its note (the book menu's scroll):

```
cargo run --release -p oblivion-assets --bin oblivion-extract -- bench meshes/clutter/magesguild/mortarpestle
cargo run --release -p oblivion-assets --bin oblivion-extract -- export-ui fonts textures/menus/alchemy textures/menus/book textures/menus/icons/magic textures/menus/icons/clutter
```

`oblivion-extract` finds the game as the tools do (`--game <folder>` names it). Then build the
adapter and the framework's runtime and install them:

```
cargo build --release -p rustvr-oblivion --target i686-pc-windows-msvc
cargo build --release --manifest-path framework/Cargo.toml --target-dir target -p rustvr-runtime -p rustvr-range
python tools/install_oblivion.py
```

The installer copies `dinput8.dll` beside `Oblivion.exe` (another one in the way goes into
`rustvr_backup/`) and writes `rustvr-runtime.txt` there, pointing at `rustvr-play.exe`;
`--uninstall` takes it out again. It doesn't build anything. Add `-p rustvr-xrsim` for the
emulated headset the tests use, and `-p rustvr-voice` for the voice plugin, which nothing builds
by default. The developer workflow (the fast boot into a test save, the test saves, worktrees and
the game lock) is in [DEVELOPING.md](DEVELOPING.md).

The adapter's own, in the game's folder; every key is optional (`crates/oblivion/src/settings.rs`).

|---|---|---|
| `d3d9on12` | true | Make the device on D3D9On12 (off: plain Direct3D 9, no VR) |

| `eye_width`, `eye_height` | 0 | The per-eye size to render at (0: the size the runtime asks for, "Resolution" above) |
| `menu_background` | 1 | How opaque the menus' parchment is, of what the game makes it (1: solid, as the game draws it) |

| `menu_spot` | "" | Its place: "imperial-city", "cloud-ruler", "dive-rock", "anvil", "rumare-night", "first-view", or several separated by commas, one each time the game starts (empty: the Imperial City; `RUSTVR_OBLIVION_MENU_SPOT` overrides it) |
