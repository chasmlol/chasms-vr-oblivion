# Oblivion in the Chasms VR Framework

The adapter (`crates/oblivion`, built as a 32-bit `dinput8.dll` beside `Oblivion.exe`) puts
Oblivion's world in the headset and the framework's player in it. Oblivion runs its world, and the
runtime runs the player: the head, the hands, the body and what they hold. The adapter only
translates. Where the adapter hooks the game is at the end, under "Hooks".
[research/oblivion-engine.md](research/oblivion-engine.md).

## How it works

| Part | What it does | Where |
|---|---|---|
| The picture | Oblivion's Direct3D 9 device is made on D3D9On12. After each eye's render, the back buffer goes through a shared Direct3D 12 texture and fence into the runtime's eye texture. | `d3d9.rs` |
| The eyes | The world render runs once per eye each frame, with the camera at that eye's pose and the eye's own asymmetric frustum. The game's first-person arms aren't drawn. | `render.rs`, `camera.rs` |
| Resolution | The game renders each eye at the headset's eye size as the runtime asks for it (its recommended size times the runtime's Resolution scale): the window, back buffer and every render target are made at that size. | `resolution.rs` |
| Depth | Each eye's depth buffer (resolved from its samples under the game's antialiasing) goes to the runtime as linear depth, so the hands, the body and what they hold are hidden behind the world. | `d3d9.rs`, `depth.rs` |
| Collision | The runtime's physics gets the world Oblivion's own Havok has: every fixed body's shapes near the player (the land, buildings, rocks, trees, furniture) as triangles, so what the hands hold, drop and throw stops at it and lands on it. | `collision.rs`, `docs/research/oblivion-havok.md` |
| The HUD | After the eyes, the game draws its HUD and menus into an alpha render target of ours (not its back buffer), alpha blended as coverage: the HUD texture is the UI premultiplied, over nothing. The HUD is cut from its own menus' tiles: the bars, the weapon and the spell, the compass and the active effects over the off-hand wrist while its palm is turned up, the reticle and the card of what it's on where the head looks, a foe's health over the foe, subtitles and notices on the caption strip. Outside the world, the whole picture is a menu panel. | `render.rs`, `d3d9.rs`, `hud.rs`, `tiles.rs` |
| The player | Before each game frame, the player faces where the head looks (rotation and pitch). A third-person view goes back to first person. | `player.rs` |
| Input | The game's DirectInput keyboard and mouse read the runtime's actions, through the player's own `Oblivion.ini` bindings. While in VR the real keyboard and mouse don't reach the game. | `input.rs` |
| The window | It's always "active", so the game never pauses for focus. In the test mode it's off-screen and never activated. | `window.rs` |
| Settings | `Oblivion.ini` is windowed and skips the intro films while in VR. The player's values are kept and put back. | `game_settings.rs` |
| The body | The runtime draws the player's body from the game's own models: the race's skin where nothing is worn, the armour and clothes worn over it, on the game's skeleton. The head: the race's head and the parts made to it (ears, mouth, teeth, tongue, eyes), the hair (unless a helmet or hood covers its slot) and what's worn on the head, each with the race's, the eyes' or the hair's own texture. The face: FaceGen's coordinates (the race's plus the NPC's), the age map FaceGen's age picks, the hair's colour; the runtime morphs the head's parts and makes its texture as the game does. In first person the head stays out of the player's eyes. Its joints keep their volume, and the armour's plates move as the hard things they are, as `content/armour/oblivion.toml` marks them (every vanilla set; the framework's `docs/ARMOUR.md`, `docs/research/armour.md`). | `body.rs`, `docs/research/mirror.md`, `content/armour/oblivion.toml` |
| The mirror | A standing mirror the player calls up (the runtime's Body tab, or pulling the gripped hands apart in front of the chest). The game's world in it is one more render a frame from the head reflected in the glass, its near plane on the glass, copied with its depth; the runtime draws each eye's reflection with the player in it. | `render.rs`, `d3d9.rs` |
| The race menu | Opened by a new game or `showracemenu`: the world stays round the player, a mirror stands in front of them and the menu (the part of the picture its tiles take) beside it, worked by the laser. The game's own third-person player is hidden; every change in the menu (race, sex, face, hair, eyes) shows on the mirrored body as it's made. | `menus.rs`, `hud_info.rs`, `link.rs` |
| Lighting | The body and what the hands hold are lit as Oblivion's own shaders light them: the scene's sun or moon, the point lights near the player (torches, candles, spells), the ambient (Night-Eye's too), normal maps, specular, the skin's rim, glow maps and the fog. | `lighting.rs`, `docs/research/oblivion-lighting.md` |
| Weapons | The equipped weapon (the game's model) hangs at the left hip, or on the back if two-handed, and the hand draws it. The equipped shield is taken from over the left shoulder. Edges, points, heads and guards are measured on each model. | `weapons.rs` |
| Archery | The equipped bow is the runtime's bow (the game's model, measured on it, bending as the game's own morph bends it, its limbs springing back after a shot as the player's bow animation has them), on the back over the left shoulder; the arrows come from whichever of the body's slots they're kept in (the holsters' Arrows slot over the right shoulder, where Oblivion wears its quiver, to begin with): the kind on the bow as the bow's own arrows (their model, the equipped stack's count), another kind equipped by the game as it comes out and then the bow's own in the hand. The body wears the quiver with as many of its five arrows showing as are left. An arrow loosed is the game's own `ArrowProjectile` from the VR arrow's tip along it, with the power the game gives its bow held drawn that long (the draw stands in for the hold timer), then the game's `UseAmmo`, fatigue, invisibility and wear; `WPNBowDraw` and `bowShoot` play at the bow, and held drawn the fatigue burns as the game's hold burns it. The arrow flies, hits, sticks and is picked up as the game's own. See [research/archery.md](research/archery.md) | `archery.rs`, `body.rs` |
| Combat | Hits are the game's own hit with the equipped weapon (damage, skill, enchantments). A blow met by the weapon or shield is the game's block. Spells charge in the palm and go off as the game's own cast: target spells from the palm along the aim, touch spells into what the hand touches, self spells on the player. An enchanted staff casts from its tip along its length as the game's own staff cast (its charge used up, or the game's "not enough charge"). | `combat.rs` |
| Magic | The game's own casting art where the hand casts: the spell's main effect's model playing its `SpecialIdle_Cast` art (fire's flames, frost's mist, healing's sparkles), its first part held burning in the palm while the spell is readied (small) and charging (growing), with the effect's casting light fading in and its casting sound as the charge starts; a staff's enchantment the same at its tip. An enchanted weapon or staff shimmers with its enchantment's own effect shader (its membrane, drawn over the held model), and a spell's shader on the player (healing's glow, a shield's flames, being burned) is drawn over the body and hands, through the game's own fade. | `magic.rs`, `scene.rs`, `docs/research/torches.md` |
| Torch | The equipped torch is on the body as the equipped weapon is, flame up and burning: drawn from a holsters slot (the Torches category), the game equips it and it takes the copy's place in the hand; equipped from the game's menu, it starts at the right hip. Either hand draws it, waves it, holsters it in any holster and drops it (it lies burning where it lands, as a sword let go lies). The game draws it: a copy of its model with the game's flame, its light made from the torch's form (radius, colour, attenuation) and flickered as the game flickers a torch, lighting the world, the body and the hands; it crackles with the game's held-torch loop. | `torch.rs`, `scene.rs` |
| Grabbing people | A hand on any part of an actor is the game's own grab: a `bhkMouseSpringAction` on the Havok body of the bone held, at the point held, pulled where the hand would have it with the hand's pull (in the game's masses: its ragdolls weigh about eight times a person, measured on the player's own). A dead or knocked-down body drags by any part. A living one keeps animating: the held limb's bones go physical (their blend objects' gains), the spine partly, and blend back when let go; pulled along the ground it's pushed after the pull (its character controller's push). Lifted by the head, neck or torso, or yanked (a leg trips sooner), it's knocked down (the game's `KnockbackActor`) and hangs from the hand, kept from getting up while held; let go, it gets up the game's way. The throat squeezed drains fatigue until the game knocks it out; held up by the throat it claws at the hand and kicks. Choking, knocking down, throwing or holding a living actor long is the game's `OnAttacked` from the player (its assault crime and bounty, combat). An open hand pushes a body that's all physics, and a hand coming in fast shoves a standing one. | `grabs.rs`, `docs/research/npcs.md` |
| Things in the world | Everything the player can take near them (weapons, armour, clothes, books, potions, ingredients, keys, soul gems, sigil stones, apparatus, carriable lights, clutter) is the hands' to take or pull with the gloves: its collision its own Havok shape, its weight its Havok mass, drawn by the game where the hand has it (a candle still burns), and let go, the game's again, flying on with the throw. A card beside it says what it is: its name, damage or armour, weight, value, condition, and whether it's stolen. Let go in any body slot (a hip, a shoulder, the chest, the belt), the player takes it: the game's own pick-up, with its stealing and its message. | `items.rs`, `objects.rs` |
| Doors and levers | Every door near the player that swings or slides in place (not a load door) and every scripted activator with a moving part (levers, switches, buttons, wheels) is a hinge or a slide in the runtime's physics (`OBJ_VALVE`, `JOINT_PANEL`): the joint from the model's own "Open" or "Forward" animation (read from the game's archives), its shape the game's Havok shape (or its drawn shape where it has no body of its own, or its body lies off its drawing), its mass the model's or its drawn volume of its Havok material (a lid no heavier than a hand lifts by its edge; a button against a firm spring). Taken anywhere and swung, pushed with a palm, stopped by the player's body. A shut door is latched: a tug to the latch's end is the game's own Activate (its script, lock and key, open state); opened, the game's animation is stopped and the door's nodes and keyframed body follow the hand; swung shut, the game's Activate again. A lever's end of travel is the game's Activate (an animation its script plays in answer is stopped: the hand moved it). A sliding door (a portcullis, a ruin's stone) taken in a hand is tugged: the game's animation moves it. Traps and their triggers stay the game's. The game moving one itself (an NPC, the trigger at gaze) plays its animation and the runtime follows. | `world.rs`, `docs/research/world.md` |
| The lock | Picked at the lock itself: a lockpick drawn from the body (the Lockpicks slot, the left chest pocket) with its tip put to a locked door or container is the player's own Activate of it, and the game's lock (its menu: the world stops, its tumblers, timing, Security, picks breaking, crime, sounds) opens where the tip touched. Its flat picture isn't shown: its own tumbler models stand in a row at the keyhole, drawn by the game in its world at the menu's heights, and its lock level, picks and Security float beside them. The tip across the row is under a tumbler, the tip moved up pushes it, the pick hand's trigger sets it (the game's cursor, mouse and click, so every rule is the game's); pulled a hand's width out, it shuts. A is Auto Attempt, B closes it; the tumbler rising, at the top, set and a broken pick are felt. A lock opened another way stands where the eyes were on the thing. See [research/interact.md](research/interact.md). | `lock.rs` |
| Repair | A repair hammer drawn from the body (the Hammers slot, the front of the belt on the right) struck on what the player holds or wears repairs it a strike at a time: the weapon in the other hand, the shield on the arm, the armour worn where it's worn (the helmet at the head, the cuirass on the chest, the greaves on the legs, the boots at the feet, the gauntlets on the other hand). Each strike (the head arriving at 1.2 m/s, lifted off before the next) is one click of the game's own repair menu in its hammer mode on that thing, made and gone in the frame: what it restores (Armorer), the cap, the journeyman rule, the hammer breaking, the skill's use, its sounds and messages, none in combat. Felt in both hands. See [research/interact.md](research/interact.md). | `repair.rs` |
| Soul gems | When the game traps a soul (its own soul trap: the gem, its message, its art at the body), the Soul Trap effect's own projectile art flies from the body to a soul gem in the player's hand, else to the pack (the Soul Gems slot at the small of the back), and the hand feels it land. A filled gem in the hand wears Soul Trap's own enchanting shimmer (the game draws filled and empty alike). A filled gem held to the enchanted weapon in the other hand (0.6 s) is the game's own recharge with it (its recharge menu made for a moment, the weapon's row clicked). The Soul Gems slot gives the strongest soul first. | `soul.rs`, `magic.rs` |
| Enchanting | A filled gem held to the Arcane University's Altar of Enchanting (0.5 s), with the weapon or shield in the other hand held to it too, or alone, is the player's activation of the altar (its script's `ShowEnchantment`), with them chosen in its menu by its own lists; the menu floats beside the altar for the effects, name and Create. | `enchant.rs` |
| The Sigil Stone | The stone at the top of a Sigil Keep is one of the runtime's constrained props (as the chains are): taken in a hand, it comes out of its flame after the hand, the game's 3D with it; pulled 30 cm out, it's the player's activation (its script: its art, the stone into the pack, the gate's collapse). Let go early, it drifts back. | `sigil.rs` |
| The Ayleids' magic | A hand held in an Ayleid well's light (half a second) is the player's activation of the well (its script: once a day its power). A Welkynd or Varla stone in a hand, the trigger squeezed hard, is crushed: the inventory's use of it (its magicka or recharge, words, sound); one lying about is picked up by the game first. The Ayleid Stones slot is the pack's right pouch. | `ayleid.rs` |
| Shrines | A thing the shrine's own script names (its offering) held to a Daedric shrine for half a second is the player's activation of it, its own question answered yes by the laying (one lying about is taken into the pack first: its script counts the pack); its other messages are left to read. An empty hand on a wayshrine or a chapel's altar (0.6 s), or kneeling before one (the head low, facing it, a second), is its activation: its blessing or refusal. | `shrines.rs` |
| Sitting and sleeping | Sitting down for real (the head below 78% of the standing eye height) on a chair, bench or bed (its collision under the head) is the player's activation of it: the game seats them, or the bed asks its own way (its sleep and wait menu, ownership, trespass, crime). Standing up for real while seated is the game's Activate. Seated, the view's anchor stays at the standing eye's height over the feet, so the real sitting lowers it once. | `rest.rs`, `render.rs` |
| Yielding | Both hands raised over the head, apart (0.7 s), before someone the head looks at who is fighting the player (or anyone while the player has a bounty: a guard) is the game's own yield: Block held in its input, then the player's activation of them (their own accept or reject; a guard's crime dialogue). | `surrender.rs` |
| Menus | The world goes on being rendered under menus (the game's frozen picture of it is skipped), and every menu floats in it in pieces of its own UI, read from its tiles: a container's list, tabs and buttons beside the container; barter beside the merchant; dialogue at the speaker (the name over their head, the line under their face, the topics beside them); the big four ahead of the player with the HUD's switcher; questions and counts where the eyes look; services from dialogue beside the speaker; the race menu beside the mirror; anything else ahead, cut out of the screen. The parchment is a little see-through. The game's cursor follows the laser (with a tick in the hand as the game's hover moves onto another thing), the trigger clicks, the right stick turns its mouse wheel. See `docs/research/menus.md`. | `hud.rs`, `tiles.rs`, `menus.rs`, `render.rs` |
| Riding | The game's horses near the player offer their saddles (measured on the game's `saddle.nif`, on each horse's live spine): a hand closing on one is the player's own activation of the horse, so the game's mount happens (whose horse, the crime of stealing it, its animation and sounds). Riding, the runtime's reins, stick and spurs give the game its own controls (the joystick's Y the pace, X the turn, Run the gallop, Jump the jump); the horse's turn carries the view round, the view's anchor is the rider's eye held steady on it, and the body sits in the saddle with its legs round the horse's own body. Getting off is the same activation. No fighting from the saddle (the game's rule). | `riding.rs`, `docs/design/riding.md` |
| Books and notes | A book or note lying about, taken in a hand, is read at once: the game reads it as activating it does (its book menu, a skill book's skill rise, a note's script, its Take with the stealing), once while it's held (a magic scroll isn't: the game would take it). The book menu floats over the book in the hand, turned to the eyes however the hand turns it, steadied (no shake with the hand), the game's two pages tipped a little about the spine (a note's scroll in one piece), drawn with the hands in their depth; the free hand's grip near the pages, dragged across them, turns them (a page every 5 cm; the game's own page step and sound), and its fingertip presses the buttons (Take, Exit, the page arrows), no laser. Let go of, or Exit: shut (it opens again once it's let go of and taken again). It isn't a menu: opened with no menu up it's kept off the interface's menu stack, so the game plays on (walking, fighting, the HUD but what the book covers), and its pages and buttons are worked on the menu itself (its own page step and click), the game's mouse being its play. A book activated on a shelf by looking at it comes to the off hand and opens there; one read from the inventory is held in the off hand like a sheet. See `docs/design/books.md`. | `books.rs`, `items.rs`, `hud.rs` |
| The map | Off for now: the folded map is on the body only with `map_slot` set (the body's slots are the holsters'). Kept there and drawn from it, the game's map opens (the local map inside, the world map outside) and its page and tabs are held in the hand like paper, turned to the eyes; the other hand's grip on its far side spreads it between the hands (bigger as they part), its grip on the face drags the map (the game's own pan), and its fingertip presses the tabs and places, no laser (fast travel, with the game's own question and rules). Put back or let go, it goes back to the hip and the map shuts. | `map.rs`, `hud.rs` |
| Holsters | The player's things in slots on the body: the inventory read with the game's own rows, in categories (food, potions, poisons, ingredients, scrolls, arrows, weapons, soul gems, keys, books, sigil stones, apparatus, misc; lockpicks and the Skeleton Key; repair hammers), each with what its effects restore, its value, weight and damage as the inventory shows them, and its model (`meshes\clutter` exported). A hand squeezing in a slot takes its first thing (by the slot's categories and order: most healing first, most valuable...) as the game's model in the fist, with the inventory's own sound. Held at the mouth it's eaten or drunk (the game's own `EquipItem`: its effects, swallow or chew, potion limit); a scroll held up to the eyes is read (the player's scroll use); a poison touched to the weapon in the other hand goes on it (the game's poisoning, without its box); a weapon is equipped and the hand has the game's weapon; let go in a slot it goes back; let go anywhere else the game drops it where it comes to rest. The game's own slots: healing in front of the left hip, arrows over the right shoulder, scrolls and poisons on the chest, food at the right hip, lockpicks in the left chest pocket, repair hammers at the front of the belt on the right, soul gems in the pack's left pouch at the small of the back (the strongest soul first), Welkynd and Varla stones in its right pouch. See `docs/research/holsters.md`. | `inventory.rs` |
| The holsters' editor | A menu of the game's own UI (menu type 1050, its factory hooked; an XML of its prefabs, rows, icons, buttons and fonts read by its own `ReadXML`), floating ahead in three strips: the slots, the categories of the one picked (in and out, earlier and later), and buttons (the order inside, an empty slot's tick, size, reach, worn first, Move by hand, New, Remove, Ok). Opened by a grip held in a slot or RustVR's Holsters button; B closes it. | `slot_menu.rs`, `hud.rs` |
| Sounds | Every sound the runtime makes for the hands is one of Oblivion's own sound records (their files, levels and variations), as a sound pack (`oblivion-extract sounds`, `oblivion_assets::sounds`). A thing taken, put away or dropped plays the inventory's sound of a thing of its kind and knocks into the world with the game's collision sound for its Havok material and mass, both as the game picks them (its own functions asked for each thing); the weapon on the body draws and sheathes with its type's draw and sheathe (an enchanted one's draw with its ring); the gravity gloves flick with a big weapon's swish, quiet, and fly with Telekinesis's travel (Mysticism's), and what they pull lands as anything taken does; each bite or gulp at the mouth is the game's chewing or swallowing; a slot found and RustVR's menu, the menus' focus and OK. Drawing from the body's slots, putting back and stowing are the game's own inventory sounds, so the runtime plays nothing there. | `sounds.rs`, `objects.rs` |
| Pockets | While the player sneaks, the NPCs within 3 m (alive, not fighting) wear pockets on their own bones: belt pouches at the hips, a purse at the belt's front, an inner pocket at the chest, a bag on the back for bigger things. In them is their inventory as the game's pickpocketing lists it (its own entries and filter: nothing worn), shared out by kind. A thing pulled out is the game's own attempt: its chance (both Sneaks with Luck, the value taken) against its roll, then its own transfer (stolen, its message) or its own crime and reaction (bounty, disposition, the victim's alarm, never robbable again). The session is the game's menu: closed with nothing tried, its rummaging check and a use of Sneak; with things taken, its check for witnesses. Planting is the game's rule (weightless things only). A thing out of a pocket is drawn from the game's model with the game's own card over it. `docs/research/pickpocket.md` | `pockets.rs` |
| Alchemy | Every mortar and pestle near the player is a bench: the runtime draws it as two pieces from the game's model (the mortar, a bowl the hands use; the pestle, the hands' own), the game's own model hidden. Up to four ingredients go into the bowl, each kind once (the game's menu's rules), and lie in it as themselves, shrinking as the pestle grinds them; the alembic, calcinator and retort within 75 cm count, the best of each kind. Whenever what's in the bowl changes, the game's own alchemy menu is made for a moment and gone again (no menu mode) to work out what it would make, shown on a card of its own art, icons and fonts over the mortar. All ground, its Create makes the potion (the ingredients picked up and used, the skill, the message, the sound) and the bottle rises out of the mortar to be taken; with no effect shared, the game's own failure words and sound, the ingredients lost. Not in combat (the game's rule). See [research/alchemy.md](research/alchemy.md). | `alchemy.rs` |
| Swimming | The game's water goes to the runtime each frame: the cell's water height, whether the game has the player swimming and their head under, breath against its maximum, Water Walking, and the game's own swim speed (Speed, Athletics, Swim Speed effects, load), with the water's colour and fog. While the game has the player swimming, the arms' strokes (the runtime's) are the frame's step in `Actor::Move`, the stick's swimming added, turned back by the pitch the game's swimming turns a step by; the game's character controller moves the player with its collision, holds them at its surface and lets them out at the shore. The game's joystick is nudged the way the strokes go so it sees a swimmer (its strokes, its Athletics training). The breath meter is a HUD piece where the game has it; the sky's underwater fog is decided where the headset's eyes are; a hand slapping into the water gets the game's splash and sound. Research and design: [research/swimming.md](research/swimming.md). | `swim.rs`, `movement.rs`, `player.rs` |
| Tests | `rustvr-console-test` runs timed commands from the game's frame: `LoadGame <save>`, `SelectSpell <name or id>`, `EquipAll <id>`, `PlaceInFront <ref> <metres>`, `PlayerHorse <base> [<metres>]` (a horse of the player's own beside them), `PlaceAt <base> <ahead> <right> <up> [<turn>]` (a new one of a base form there, level, the player's own, settled where it falls: a bench set out), `Place <base> <x> <y> <z> [<angle>]` (the same at a place in the cell, game units: a set-out that stays put however the player stands), `RunFile <path>` (a file of timed lines in the test file's format, queued in one go: the alchemy playtest's row of tables, `tools/alchemy-row.txt`), `OnNewest <base> <script>` (a line of script on the newest reference of a base form: one just set out, locked or owned), `SaveGame <name>` (the console's own save, for the next run), `Wear <percent>` (everything the player has on at that much of its health, to repair), `Discharge <percent>` (the equipped weapon's enchantment at that much of its charge), or any line of script (base forms by hex id). Compile errors are logged. | `console.rs` |

## Setting up

The game's models are converted once into `local_assets/oblivion` (never committed; the clutter is
what's in people's pockets):

```
cargo run --release -p oblivion-assets --bin oblivion-extract -- export meshes/weapons meshes/staffs meshes/lights meshes/characters meshes/armor meshes/clothes meshes/creatures/horse textures/characters textures/menus/misc/hud_reticle.dds meshes/clutter meshes/oblivion/sigil meshes/dungeons/misc
```

and its sounds, as the runtime's sound pack (`local_assets/oblivion/sounds`):

```
cargo run --release -p oblivion-assets --bin oblivion-extract -- sounds
```

(`meshes/characters` brings the races' heads and the parts made to them, the hair and their FaceGen
morphs, `morphs/*.rvf`, and FaceGen's age control, `facegen.toml`.)

Bows are written bent too (`#bent`, from their morph), arrows alone (`#arrow`: the plain key is
the whole model, quiver and all) and their quivers worn on the skeleton (`#quiver0` ..
`#quiver5`), with `bows.toml` (the limbs' spring, from the player's bow animation); the reticle is
the bow's optional crosshair.

For alchemy, the mortars and pestles split and measured, and the game's fonts and menu art for its
cards and its note (the book menu's scroll):

```
cargo run --release -p oblivion-assets --bin oblivion-extract -- bench meshes/clutter/magesguild/mortarpestle
cargo run --release -p oblivion-assets --bin oblivion-extract -- export-ui fonts textures/menus/alchemy textures/menus/book textures/menus/icons/magic textures/menus/icons/clutter
```

Then `python tools/install_oblivion.py` builds and installs the adapter beside `Oblivion.exe`.

## Controls

| VR | Oblivion |
|---|---|
| Left stick | Walking, where the head looks, as the game's own analog joystick walking: slow to full speed over the stick's travel, at the pace Always Run sets (pushing it clears Auto Move) |
| Left stick click (sprint) | Run, when Always Run is off |
| Right stick click, or its down flick, or ducking | Crouch/Sneak (kept in step with the game's sneaking) |
| A (either hand) | Jump (out of the water too, at a ledge) |
| Empty hands in the water, grip squeezed | Swim: the hands hold the water and the player is pulled past them, several times their pace, then glides; down to rise, up to dive (the stick swims too, where the head looks) |
| Trigger, empty hand | Activate (what the head looks at); held: Rest/Wait. The off hand, with a spell ready: a finger on it readies the spell (its art in the palm), pulling it charges it, letting go (or touching, for a touch spell) casts |
| Trigger, holding an enchanted staff | Charge its enchantment at the tip; let go to cast it along the staff |
| Grip at the torch's holster | Draw the torch (when one is equipped); let go in a holster to hang it up, anywhere else to drop it |
